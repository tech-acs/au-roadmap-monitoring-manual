# Part 1 – Getting started

## 1. What the platform does

The AU Roadmap Monitoring Platform helps AU member states track how they are implementing the **African Union Roadmap on Harnessing the Demographic Dividend through Investments in the Youth**.

Each country uses the platform to:

1. **Report indicator data** every year for the four Roadmap pillars.

2. **Record programmatic work** – the deliverables, projects and activities carried out under each pillar, with budgets and results.

3. **Share success stories** with other countries.

4. **See dashboards and scores** that show where the country is on track, where it must accelerate, and where the trend must be reversed.

5. **Compare** its performance with other countries and regions.

6. **Export reports** in CSV format for national use.

The platform is available in **English** and **French**.

## 2. Key concepts in plain language

Take two minutes to read this section. Everything else in the manual uses these words.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Pillar** | One of the four thematic areas of the Roadmap: **Employment and Entrepreneurship**, **Education and Skills Development**, **Health and Wellbeing**, and **Rights, Governance and Youth Empowerment**. |
| **Indicator** | A measurable statistic under a pillar, for example "Unemployment rate, by sex, age and persons with disabilities" (code 8.5.2). Each indicator has a code, a baseline year, a start tracking year, an end tracking year and a direction. |
| **Disaggregation** | A breakdown of an indicator that you must answer separately, for example "Female youth unemployment rate". On some screens it is called a **question**. An indicator can have one or several disaggregations. |
| **Baseline** | The value of a disaggregation in the **baseline year** (the year before tracking started, usually 2017). You enter this once. All progress is measured from this point. |
| **Common target** | The target that applies to **all countries**. It is defined centrally by the platform administrator, either as a fixed number or as a formula based on your baseline. You cannot change it. |
| **National target** | An **optional** target that your country sets for itself for a disaggregation. It is used for the National Dashboard and national scores only. |
| **Response year** | The year the data you are entering refers to. For example, if you enter the 2024 unemployment rate, the response year is 2024. |
| **Response / answer** | The actual observed value you enter for a disaggregation in a given response year, together with its data source, notes and supporting files. |
| **Response score** | The progress of one disaggregation from the baseline towards the target, expressed as a percentage. 0 = no progress, 100 = target reached. |
| **Indicator score** | The average of the response scores of an indicator's disaggregations for the year. |
| **Achievement ratio (R)** | Tells you whether you are progressing **fast enough** to reach the target by the end year. It compares progress made with time elapsed. |
| **Performance status** | The colour code derived from the achievement ratio: **Maintain** (green), **Accelerate** (yellow) or **Reverse** (red). |
| **Pillar score** | The average of the indicator scores in a pillar for the year. |
| **Overall country score** | The geometric mean of the four pillar scores. It is only calculated when all four pillars have a score. |
| **Deliverable** | A concrete output your country has committed to under a pillar (for example "National Youth Employment Strategy"). |
| **Project / activity** | A programme or activity that contributes to a deliverable, with a period, a budget and results. |
| **Success story** | A short article describing a positive result or good practice, which can be published on the public website. |

## 3. Logging in and out

Accounts are created by the platform administrator or by your country administrator. **There is no self-registration.** If you do not have an account, ask your country focal point.

### 3.1 Log in

1. Open the platform address in your web browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox are recommended). Your focal point will give you the address.

2. On the public home page, click **Sign In** at the top right.

3. On the **Login** page, enter your **Email Address** and **Password**.

4. Click **Login**.

5. You land on your country's **Per Country** dashboard.
> **Tip** – Bookmark the login page so you can return quickly.

### 3.2 Forgot your password?

1. On the **Login** page click **Forgot Your Password?**.

2. Enter your **Email Address** and click **Send Password Reset Link**.

3. Open the email you receive and click the link.

4. Choose a new password (at least 8 characters) and confirm it.

5. Log in with the new password.
> **Note** – If the email does not arrive within a few minutes, check your spam folder. If it still does not arrive, ask your administrator to reset the password for you.

### 3.3 Log out

1. Click the **user icon** (round icon at the top right).

2. Click **Logout**.
> **Important** – Always log out when you use a shared computer.

## 4. Switching the language (English / French)

1. Click the round **EN** or **FR** badge at the top right of the page.

2. Choose **English** or **Français**.

The whole interface, including pillar and indicator names, switches language. Your choice is remembered on this browser for one year. The language switch is also available on the public website before you log in.

## 5. Your profile and changing your password

1. Click the **user icon** at the top right and choose **Profile**.

2. The **Account Details** panel shows your **Name**, **Email**, **Country** and **Roles**. These are set by the administrator; if something is wrong, ask them to correct it.

3. To change your password, use the **Password & Security** panel:

   1. Type your **Current Password**.

   2. Type a **New Password** (at least 8 characters).

   3. Retype it in **Confirm New Password**.

   4. Click **Update Password**.

4. The message **Your password has been updated successfully** confirms the change.
> **Tip** – Use a password that you do not use anywhere else and never share it.

### 6.2 What each menu item is for

The menu only shows the pages your role allows. A standard **Country Admin** sees the items below. Your administrator may grant additional items such as **Reports**, **Report Builder** or **Success Stories**.

| Menu item | Section of menu | Use it to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillars Dashboard** | Dashboards | See your country's programmatic table and indicator scores per pillar and per year, with a full score breakdown. |
| **National Dashboard** | Dashboards | The same view, but measured against your **national** targets. |
| **Per Country** | Dashboards | Your home page: pillar cards, quick stats, indicator list and recent activity for one year. |
| **Country Comparison** | Dashboards | Compare your country with other countries over one or several years. |
| **Regional Comparison** | Dashboards | Compare regions (and countries within them). |
| **Deliverables** | Apps | Record deliverables, projects and activities; open success stories per pillar. |
| **Indicators** | Apps | **Enter indicator data**: baselines, national targets and yearly responses. |
| **Reports** / **Report Builder** | Apps (if enabled) | Download CSV reports and build custom ones. |
| **Success Stories** | Apps (if enabled) | Manage all success stories. |
| **Users & Roles → Users** | Set up | Create and manage user accounts for your country. |
